NCERT Section 13.3 • Step 01

13.3 Size of the Nucleus

How did physicists measure something a hundred thousand times smaller than an atom? Let us explore Rutherford's pioneer postulate, Geiger and Marsden's α-particle scattering, fast-electron bombardment, and the remarkable discovery that all nuclei have the same constant density!

Key Relation (Eq. 13.5)

R = R0 A1/3

R0 = 1.2 × 10−15 m = 1.2 fm

Geiger–Marsden Expt

1. Distance of Closest Approach

Rutherford was the pioneer who postulated and established the existence of the atomic nucleus. At his suggestion, Geiger and Marsden performed their classic experiment on the scattering of α-particles from thin gold foils.

Key Experimental Fact:

For an α-particle of kinetic energy 5.5 MeV approaching a gold nucleus, the distance of closest approach is about 4.0 × 10−14 m.

Since pure Coulomb repulsive force was solely responsible for scattering and positive charge is confined to the nucleus, the actual nuclear size must be < 4.0 × 10−14 m.

Beyond Coulomb Force

2. Probing True Nuclear Size

What happens if we use α-particles of higher energies (> 5.5 MeV)? The distance of closest approach becomes smaller, and at some point the scattering begins to be affected by short-range attractive nuclear forces, differing from Rutherford's pure Coulomb calculations!

Fast Electron Bombardment:

From the distance at which deviations set in, nuclear sizes can be inferred. By performing scattering experiments using fast electrons instead of α-particles as projectiles bombarding targets of various elements, the sizes of nuclei have been accurately measured.

Liquid Drop Analogy

3. Constant Nuclear Density

Since radius R = R0A1/3, the volume of a spherical nucleus is proportional to R3, which is directly proportional to mass number A:

V = (4/3)πR3 = (4/3)πR03A ∝ A

Because both mass (≈ A·mu) and volume (∝ A) are proportional to A, the density of the nucleus is constant and independent of A for all nuclei!

ρnucleus ≈ 2.3 × 1017 kg m−3 (Water = 103 kg m−3)

Mathematical Proof: Why Nuclear Matter Density (ρ) is Independent of Mass Number (A)

Step 1: Mass & Volume of Nucleus

If average mass of one nucleon is m ≈ 1.66 × 10−27 kg, then for a nucleus of mass number A:

Mass (M) = m × A

Volume (V) = 43πR3 = 43π(R0A1/3)3 = 43πR03A

Step 2: Cancel A in Density Ratio

ρ = MassVolume = m · A(4/3)πR03 · A = 3m4πR03

Notice that A cancels out completely! Different nuclei are like drops of a liquid of constant density.

Step 3: Substitute Constants

ρ = 3 × 1.66 × 10−27 kg4 × 3.1416 × (1.2 × 10−15 m)3

ρ ≈ 2.3 × 1017 kg m−3

Why so huge compared to water (103 kg m−3)? Because ordinary matter consisting of atoms has a vast amount of empty space!

NCERT Section 13.4.1 • Step 02

13.4.1 Mass – Energy Equivalence

Before the advent of Einstein's theory of special relativity, it was presumed that mass and energy were conserved separately in a reaction. However, Einstein showed that mass is another form of energy and one can convert mass-energy into other forms of energy (say kinetic energy) and vice-versa!

Einstein's Relation (Eq. 13.6)

E = m c2

c ≈ 3 × 108 m s−1 (in vacuum)

NCERT Example 13.2 Page 310 • Textbook Solved

Question: Calculate the energy equivalent of 1 g of substance.

Solution:

Given mass, m = 1 g = 10−3 kg

Speed of light, c = 3 × 108 m s−1

Energy, E = m c2 = 10−3 × (3 × 108)2 J

E = 10−3 × 9 × 1016 = 9 × 1013 J

"Thus, if one gram of matter is converted to energy, there is a release of an enormous amount of energy."

Nuclear Scale Conversion (Example 13.3) 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²

Energy Equivalent of 1 Atomic Mass Unit (1 u)

1 u = 1.6605 × 10−27 kg

E = 1.6605 × 10−27 × (2.9979 × 108)2 kg m2/s2

E = 1.4924 × 10−10 J = 1.4924 × 10−101.602 × 10−19 eV

E = 0.9315 × 109 eV = 931.5 MeV  ⇒  1 u = 931.5 MeV/c2

Experimental Verification: Achieved in the study of nuclear reactions amongst nucleons, nuclei, electrons and other particles. In any reaction, the conservation law of energy states that initial energy equals final energy provided the energy associated with mass is also included!

NCERT Section 13.4.2 • Step 03

13.4.2 Nuclear Binding Energy & Mass Defect

A nucleus is made up of neutrons and protons. One might expect the mass of the nucleus to equal the total mass of its individual protons and neutrons. However, the actual nuclear mass M is found to be always less than the sum of its constituents! Where does the missing mass go?

Mass Defect (Eq. 13.7)

ΔM = [Z mp + (A − Z)mn] − M

Binding Energy (13.8)

Eb = ΔM c2

Per Nucleon (13.9)

Ebn = Eb / A

NCERT Case Study: Mass Defect of Oxygen-16 (168O)

8 p⁺, 8 n⁰, 8 e⁻

Let us consider 168O, a nucleus which has 8 neutrons and 8 protons:

Mass of 8 neutrons = 8 × 1.00866 u = 8.06928 u
Mass of 8 protons  = 8 × 1.00727 u = 8.05816 u
(Note: Mass of 8 electrons = 8 × 0.00055 u = 0.00440 u)
Expected mass of 168O nucleus (8 × 2.01593 u): = 16.12744 u

• Atomic mass of 168O from mass spectroscopy = 15.99493 u

• Subtracting mass of 8 electrons (0.00440 u), experimental mass of 168O nucleus:

Experimental Mass of 168O Nucleus (M): = 15.99053 u
Mass Defect (ΔM = 16.12744 u − 15.99053 u): = 0.13691 u

NCERT Example 13.3 Result for 168O:

Since 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c2, for 168O: ΔM = 0.13691 × 931.5 MeV/c2 = 127.5 MeV/c2. Therefore, the energy needed to separate 168O into its constituents is Eb = 127.5 MeV (and Ebn = 127.5 / 16 = 7.97 MeV/nucleon).

Physical Meaning of Eb and Ebn

1. Breaking a Nucleus (Endothermic)

Since the mass of the bound nucleus is less than the sum of its constituents in the unbound state, its equivalent energy is lower. If one wants to break the nucleus into Z protons and (A−Z) neutrons, this extra energy Eb = ΔM c2 has to be supplied from outside!

2. Forming a Nucleus (Exothermic)

Conversely, if a certain number of neutrons and protons are brought together to form a nucleus of a certain charge and mass, an energy Eb will be released in the process!

3. Binding Energy per Nucleon (Ebn)

A more useful measure of how well a nucleus is held together is the binding energy per nucleon (Ebn = Eb / A), which is the average energy per nucleon needed to separate a nucleus into its individual nucleons.

Observations from Figure 13.1

Two Main Features of the Plot

(i) Constancy in Middle Mass Region (30 < A < 170)

The binding energy per nucleon, Ebn, is practically constant, i.e., practically independent of the atomic mass number for nuclei of middle mass number (30 < A < 170).

  • Maximum of about 8.75 MeV for A = 56 (56Fe).
  • Drops slowly to 7.6 MeV for A = 238 (238U).

(ii) Lower Ebn at Both Extremes

Ebn is lower for both light nuclei (A < 30) and heavy nuclei (A > 170).

Also notice characteristic peaks for 4He, 12C, 16O which are extra stable compared to their immediate neighbours (like 6Li, 14N).

Crucial Board Exam Topic

Four Major Conclusions Drawn from the Curve

(i) Strong & Attractive Force

The nuclear force is attractive and sufficiently strong to produce a binding energy of a few MeV per nucleon (millions of times stronger than atomic Coulomb binding of a few eV).

(ii) Saturation Property (30 < A < 170)

Nuclear force is short-ranged. A nucleon inside a large nucleus interacts only with a maximum of p immediate neighbours within force range, contributing ≈ pk to binding energy. Adding more nucleons doesn't change the binding of interior nucleons, so Ebn ≈ pk = constant!

(iii) Nuclear Fission (Heavy Nuclei)

A very heavy nucleus (say A = 240) has lower Ebn compared to A = 120. If A = 240 breaks into two A = 120 nuclei, nucleons get more tightly bound and energy is released (Fission).

(iv) Nuclear Fusion (Light Nuclei A ≤ 10)

When two very light nuclei (A ≤ 10) join to form a heavier nucleus, the Ebn of the fused nucleus is greater than the lighter nuclei. The final system is more tightly bound, releasing huge energy (Fusion in the Sun).

Deep-Dive: Conclusion (ii)

Why Ebn ≈ p·k is Constant: Saturation of Nuclear Force

Consider a particular nucleon inside a sufficiently large nucleus. Because the nuclear force is short-ranged (~2–3 fm), it is under the influence of only its immediate p neighbours (shown in green). Any nucleon beyond this range (grey) has zero influence on it!
